01 · Introduction

What is yoga?

The word “Yoga” comes from the Sanskrit root yuj, meaning “join” or “unite”. It refers to the union of body, mind and soul, and to the practices that support this integration.

Yoga is a way of life that includes ethical principles, postures, breathing practices, sense-withdrawal and meditation. In school education, yoga can encourage physical fitness, mental development and emotional stability.

“A steady practice nurtures strength, calm, concentration and balance.”

Why practise yoga?

  • Develop strength, stamina, endurance and flexibility.
  • Support concentration, calmness and emotional balance.
  • Build body awareness, coordination and healthy habits.
  • Encourage a positive and balanced lifestyle.
02 · Background

A brief history

Yoga is an ancient Indian tradition. A systematic reference is found in Patanjali’s Yoga Darshana, where Maharishi Patanjali systematized yogic practices. Later sages and yogis also contributed to its development.

📜

Ancient roots

Yoga developed as an inner science with methods for understanding the connection between body and mind.

🪷

Many practices

Its practices include Yama, Niyama, Asana, Pranayama, Pratyahara, Kriya and meditation.

🌍

Worldwide practice

Yoga is now practised in many parts of the world as a path to wellbeing and self-development.

03 · Foundations

Common yogic practices

Yoga includes several connected practices. The following are introduced in the project material.

🤝

Yama & Niyama

Initial principles concerned with personal and social conduct, ethics and values.

🧍

Asana

A posture that is comfortable and can be maintained steadily. It supports stability and comfort at physical and mental levels.

🌬️

Pranayama

Breathing techniques related to awareness and regulation of the breathing process.

🌿

Pratyahara

Withdrawal of awareness from external sense experiences and turning attention inward.

🫧

Shatkarma / Kriya

Six cleansing practices described in Hatha-yogic texts: Neti, Dhauti, Basti, Trataka, Nauli and Kapalabhati.

🧘

Meditation

A relaxation practice that focuses attention, for example on the breath, and develops inner awareness.

👐

Bandha & Mudra

Practices involving control or positioning of certain muscles and parts of the body.

04 · Practice safely

General guidelines

Begin simply, practise regularly and respect your own capacity. The source material recommends learning under an experienced teacher.

Before you begin

  • Choose a clean, quiet, well-ventilated place.
  • Use a mat or blanket rather than a hard surface.
  • Wear loose, comfortable clothing.
  • Practise preferably in the early morning; an evening session may be done on an empty stomach.
  • Empty the bladder and bowels before practice.
  • Prepare the body with gentle joint movements.

During your practice

  • Start in a quiet mood and proceed without hurry.
  • Coordinate breath and movement; breathe naturally unless instructed otherwise.
  • Avoid jerks, undue force and competition.
  • Build duration and difficulty gradually.
  • Stop within your comfort and ability; do not force a posture.
  • Practise under an experienced teacher's guidance.

Important: The project advises people with chronic conditions or other health concerns to inform their teacher before starting. Special-needs students should practise with guidance appropriate to their capacity. Kriyas should be learned under expert supervision.

05 · Sun salutation

Surya Namaskara

“Surya” means Sun and “Namaskara” means salutation. Surya Namaskara is a sequence of 12 physical postures that stretches muscles and the spinal column and develops flexibility.

Prayer pose

Stand erect with feet together. Join palms in front of the chest.

Raised arms

Inhale, raise both arms overhead and gently bend the trunk backward.

Forward bend

Exhale and bend forward from the waist, bringing hands toward the floor.

Right leg back

Extend the right leg behind; bend the left knee and keep the left foot between the palms.

Mountain pose

Bring the left leg back and lift the hips, forming a triangle with the floor.

Eight-point pose

Lower knees, chest and chin toward the floor while keeping the hips raised.

Cobra pose

Lower the hips, push the chest forward and raise the trunk while inhaling.

Mountain pose

Exhale, lift the hips and bring the head between the arms.

Left leg forward

Bring the left foot forward between the palms, with the right leg extended back.

Forward bend

Bring the right foot forward, straighten the legs and bend toward the knees.

Raised arms

Inhale, raise the arms and trunk slowly, gently bending backward.

Prayer pose

Exhale, stand erect and bring the palms together at the chest.

Benefits described in the project

  • Helps develop strength, endurance and flexibility.
  • Supports concentration and energy.
  • Warms the body and improves circulation.
  • Provides flexibility to the whole body.

Do’s & don’ts

  • Synchronise breathing with body movements.
  • Inhale during upward bending and exhale during forward bending.
  • Do not practise beyond your capacity.
  • The source advises people with spinal cord injury not to practise this sequence.
06 · Foundational postures

Asanas for health

Explore the postures described in the mini-project, including their steps, reminders and limitations.

Standing posture

Tadasana · Palm Tree Pose

Stand erect with feet together and arms by the thighs. Stretch both arms upward, raise the heels and balance on the toes. Return heels to the floor, lower the arms and relax.

Remember: Keep head, neck and body aligned; do not bend forward or backward.

Benefits in source: Vertical stretch; strengthens thighs, knees and ankles.

Limitations: Avoid with a reeling sensation or painful/stiff knee and ankle joints.

Balancing posture

Vrikshasana · Tree Pose

Stand with feet together. Bend one knee and place the sole of that foot against the inside of the opposite thigh. Balance and raise the arms overhead or join palms at the chest. Repeat on the other side.

Remember: Focus on a fixed point and keep the body upright.

Benefits in source: Supports concentration, balance, coordination and leg muscle tone.

Limitations: Avoid if you experience a reeling sensation.

Balancing posture

Utkatasana

Stand with feet comfortably apart. Raise the arms in front to shoulder level, rise onto the toes and slowly lower into the described toe-balanced position. Release slowly and regain an erect stance.

Remember: Maintain balance and do not bend forward.

Benefits in source: Works the legs, arms, shoulders, pelvis and lower back; supports joint mobility.

Limitations: Avoid with a reeling sensation or painful/stiff knee and ankle joints.

Meditative posture

Vajrasana

From sitting with legs extended, fold one leg and place the foot under its buttock; repeat with the other leg. Sit between the heels with hands on the knees and spine erect.

Remember: Keep the spine erect and avoid bending forward or backward.

Benefits in source: Supports concentration and strengthens thigh and calf muscles.

Limitations: The source advises against this posture for people with chronic knee pain.

Meditative posture

Swastikasana · Auspicious Pose

Sit with legs extended. Place the left heel near the right groin and the right heel near the left groin. Settle into a comfortable cross-legged position with the spine erect and hands in jnana mudra.

Remember: Keep the spine erect; do not use force to assume the posture.

Benefits in source: Supports concentration and flexibility in knee and ankle joints.

Limitations: Avoid with sciatica or pain in knee and ankle joints.

Meditative posture

Ardhapadmasana · Half Lotus

Sit with legs extended. Fold one leg and place its sole against the opposite inner thigh; fold the other leg and place that foot on the opposite thigh. Rest hands on the knees in jnana mudra.

Remember: Keep the spine erect and do not force the legs into position.

Benefits in source: Supports concentration and is described as helping relieve mental and physical fatigue.

Limitations: Avoid with sciatica or pain in knee and ankle joints.

07 · Everyday wellness

Make practice a habit

The project emphasises regularity, patience, healthy food and adequate sleep. Begin with simple practices and gradually progress.

📅

Be consistent

Regular practice is essential. If you pause, restart with basic practices and move forward gradually.

🥗

Eat well

Along with yogic practices, take nutritious and healthy food.

🌙

Rest well

The project recommends at least eight hours of sound sleep.
